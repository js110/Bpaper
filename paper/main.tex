\begin{abstract}
Mobile crowdsensing can leak location even when exact coordinates remain on-device: a linkable report certifies presence in a task region, while silence can also shift an observer's belief. We formalize this sequential report/silence channel and introduce branch-safe participation (BSP), an online gate that selects the largest participation probability whose report and silence posteriors both satisfy a declared concentration cap under a specified mobility and completion model. Robust BSP extends this construction to a finite model set by intersecting member-wise feasible gates. We then characterize the unavoidable disclosure floor of truthful positive reports and develop disclosure-floor-aware variants that relax only the report branch while preserving the strict silence constraint. Across exploratory simulations on synthetic mobility and model-input-isolated Microsoft GeoLife traces, robust filtering reduces several modeled mismatch violations, but often at a substantial cost in simulated eligible-and-available opportunity retention, especially for small task regions. Additional stresses with spatially varying availability, nonstationary mobility, and two-step probe planning further delineate where the finite-model guarantee transfers and where it does not. A separate report-side ceiling restores part of the fine-region opportunity retention only by making that privacy relaxation explicit. Accordingly, all empirical conclusions are conditional on the binary report/silence channel, the stated availability process, and the declared finite model set; GeoLife supplies mobility traces only, not task-process, device, or production-service measurements.
\end{abstract}

\IEEEraisesectionheading{\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{obile} crowdsensing (MCS) turns personal mobile devices into a distributed sensing substrate for traffic, environmental, and urban-computing services. Keeping precise coordinates on-device is an important privacy boundary, but it does not make participation itself private. When a task is valid only within a geographic region, a truthful completion certifies that the user was eligible there; conversely, silence can also shift the platform's belief once delivery, willingness, deadline success, and privacy filtering are taken into account.

The risk is sequential rather than one-shot. A platform can link outcomes to the same account, combine them with a mobility model, and choose future tasks from the accumulated public history. The privacy problem is therefore not simply whether a coordinate is uploaded or a task assignment is hidden; it is whether an online participation decision can remain useful when both of its observable outcomes---report and silence---feed the next round of inference.

Most existing MCS privacy mechanisms intervene at a different interface: they protect coordinates, recruitment, matching, or released sensing values before or during assignment~\cite{itam2021,taskloc2022,pmta2024,ppur2024,lrppsta2025,mtadprh2025}. TMarkov~\cite{tmarkov}, for example, models time-varying mobility to construct anonymous candidate locations and a predicted trajectory in place of the actual one; its threat model also discusses location inference from accepted and rejected task locations. HECTA~\cite{inference2026}, by contrast, obscures genuine choices within task-selection sequences. In our setting, by contrast, task regions remain public, successful reports remain truthful, and the platform observes a linkable report/silence transcript. This leaves a narrower but distinct design question: \emph{how much participation can a mobile client safely expose when the binary outcome itself remains visible?}

This apparently simple binary channel creates three coupled difficulties. First, suppressing reports does not eliminate leakage because silence is itself probabilistic evidence. Second, a gate calibrated to one mobility or completion model may fail when the observer uses a different plausible model. Third, a truthful positive report has zero support outside the task region, which creates a region-dependent disclosure floor that no positive scalar thinning probability can remove.

These observations lead to branch-safe participation (BSP). Under a declared model, BSP derives the largest scalar participation gate that bounds posterior location concentration after every positive-probability output. Robust BSP (R-BSP) then intersects the feasible gates across a finite ambiguity set, making model uncertainty part of the mechanism rather than an implicit assumption. The remaining structural limitation---the truthful-report disclosure floor---is handled separately by disclosure-floor-aware BSP (DF-BSP), which introduces a report-side ceiling while retaining the original silence cap; RDF-BSP combines this asymmetric contract with finite-model robustness. Throughout, the gate is computed client-side from public task metadata and public history, while the true location is used only for local eligibility.

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalize a linkable binary task-outcome channel for sequential MCS and derive a maximal online BSP gate that constrains both report and silence posteriors under explicit mobility and completion assumptions.
    \item We extend the gate to finite model sets and evaluate informed model-mismatch attacks and outside-set boundary cases, making the scope and failure modes of robustness observable rather than implicit.
    \item We prove that truthful positive reports induce an unavoidable region-specific disclosure floor and develop DF-BSP/RDF-BSP to separate this report-side floor from the strict silence constraint.
    \item We evaluate the mechanisms as a channel-specific simulation study on synthetic mobility and model-input-group-isolated GeoLife traces, including model mismatch, outside-set and nonstationary stresses, spatially varying availability, grid sensitivity, and deeper probe planning. PML~\cite{pml2024} and PRIVIC~\cite{privic2024} are used only as task-channel diagnostics because their original output semantics and guarantees differ from ours.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper first positions the observation channel against related MCS privacy mechanisms, then develops the system model and participation gates, analyzes their guarantees and limitations, and finally evaluates privacy--service tradeoffs under matched and mismatched mobile-inference models.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Related MCS privacy work can be organized by the interface it protects. Bilateral and task-location schemes protect worker/requester or task coordinates~\cite{itam2021,taskloc2022}; other approaches use differential obfuscation~\cite{pmta2024}, secret-sharing recruitment~\cite{ppur2024}, encrypted location/reward optimization~\cite{lrppsta2025}, and local differential privacy or geo-indistinguishability~\cite{mtadprh2025}. More recent work extends these ideas to bilateral location-fingerprint assignment~\cite{fingerprint}, temporal--spatial differential privacy~\cite{tasp2025}, mobility-prediction-aware perturbation~\cite{p2taskmp2025}, encrypted quality-aware allocation~\cite{qpta2024}, federated local task selection~\cite{fedsense2025}, and blockchain/ZKP protocols~\cite{blockchainpmc2026}. The common distinction is that these mechanisms protect coordinate, matching, recruitment, release, or transaction interfaces; their guarantees and reported overhead therefore do not transfer directly to a visible truthful report/silence channel.

Among sequence-aware mechanisms, HECTA is a particularly relevant precedent because it addresses inference from task-selection sequences~\cite{inference2026}. HECTA masks genuine choices among dummy tasks through cryptographic processing and dummy-task mixing under a non-collusion assumption between the service provider and an auxiliary party. BSP takes the opposite observation boundary: task regions and linkable truthful completion outcomes remain visible, and the mechanism regulates the Bayesian posterior induced by those outputs. Because the trust model, observed transcript, and privacy metric differ, the two guarantees are not directly comparable; we therefore do not reproduce HECTA as a numerical baseline.

Bayesian privacy measures prior-to-posterior belief change in a general mechanism-design setting using KL divergence~\cite{bayesian}; it does not specify the MCS report/silence channel studied here. Adaptive differential privacy concerns a different formal guarantee~\cite{adaptive2023}, while PRIVIC protects randomized location releases under geo-indistinguishability~\cite{privic2024}. BSP instead limits posterior concentration under an explicit mobility and availability model. Because a truthful successful report has zero support outside its task region, BSP is not a differentially private coordinate-release mechanism. Privacy-preserving assignment and matching systems such as iTAM~\cite{itam2021} and anonymous task matching~\cite{shutask2018} hide worker/requester attributes or matching queries during assignment; they do not bound the posterior induced by a subsequently visible truthful completion/silence transcript. PML provides a useful optimization-based channel comparator~\cite{pml2024}. Comparator derivations and implementation checks are provided in the Supplementary Material to keep the main text focused.

Taken together, these works show why the observation interface must be made explicit before privacy guarantees are compared. Table~\ref{tab:mechanism-comparison} summarizes that distinction: iTAM and related assignment protocols hide worker/requester coordinates during assignment; TMarkov changes the shared location/trajectory interface using mobility-aware anonymous candidates; HECTA changes the visibility of task-selection sequences; PRIVIC randomizes released locations; and PML-T constrains an adapted binary task channel through pointwise leakage bounds. BSP subsumes none of these mechanisms. Its specific contribution is instead to preserve the truthful binary task outcome, bound the posterior peak on \emph{each} observable branch, and maximize the admissible scalar participation gate. The comparison therefore establishes a difference in protected interface and guarantee object, not a claim that BSP is uniformly stronger than cryptographic assignment, trajectory prediction, pointwise leakage, or geo-indistinguishability.

\section{System Model and Branch-Safe Participation}
\label{sec:model}\label{sec:method}
The distinction above suggests the central modeling requirement: the client's private sensing state must be separated from the transcript available to the platform. Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} makes this boundary explicit. Public task metadata enters both the local eligibility check and the participation filter, while the same observable report/silence outcome updates the client's declared-model belief and the attacker's inference.

\subsection{System and Observation Model}
Let $X_t\in\mathcal X$ denote a user's location cell at the sampling instant in slot $t$, where $|\mathcal X|=N$. A task specifies a public region $S_t\subseteq\mathcal X$, a sampling slot, and a deadline. A valid report requires an observation collected inside $S_t$ at that sampling instant and submitted by the deadline. Receiving or accepting a task is not sufficient. The simulator exposes each task outcome only at its common slot deadline; it does not reveal within-slot submission timing.

Let $D_t,W_t,L_t$ denote successful delivery, willingness to participate, and timely completion. In the baseline model these are independent Bernoulli variables, independent of location conditional on public history, with product probability $\alpha_t$. The filter additionally samples $G_t\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q_t)$ independently. The only published outcome is
\begin{equation}
 Y_t=\ind\{X_t\in S_t\}D_tW_tL_tG_t\in\{0,1\}.
 \label{eq:output}
\end{equation}
Here $Y_t=0$ means that no valid report appeared by the deadline. There are no separate acknowledgments of delivery, online status, refusal, or filter rejection. Reports do not disclose additional measurement content in this model. If such content, network metadata, or fine-grained submission times are visible, the observation channel must be extended before applying the guarantee.

This design represents opportunistic sampling at a specified instant, not continuous movement toward a newly assigned destination. Users do not change their paths in response to tasks. The simulated normal task stream is public and exogenous. Probe tasks share the same completion requirements as normal tasks; the evaluator's normal/probe label is not an input to the client's gate.

\subsection{Privacy Objective and Bayesian Observation Channel}
With the observation boundary fixed, the privacy problem becomes a branch-wise Bayesian update. Let $b_t(i)=\Pr(X_t=i\mid H_{t-1},S_t)$ be the predicted belief before the current output, and abbreviate $b=b_t$, $h_i=\alpha_t\ind\{i\in S_t\}$, and $m=\sum_i b_i h_i$. More generally, the branch algebra requires only a declared state-conditional success likelihood. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_t(i)&=\Pr(D_t=W_t=L_t=1 \\
&\qquad\mid X_t=i,H_{t-1},S_t),\\
h_i&=\ind\{i\in S_t\}a_t(i).
\end{aligned}
\]
If delivery, willingness, and timely completion are conditionally independent given location, public history, and the current task metadata, the joint term $a_t(i)$ factors into the product of their state-conditional probabilities. The baseline experiments use the simpler state-independent $\alpha_t$; the spatial-availability stress deliberately violates that client model to test the resulting mismatch. A gate is a deterministic function of public history and the current task; its random coin is private. For $q>0$ and $m>0$, the report posterior is
\begin{equation}
 b_i^+=\frac{b_i h_i}{m}.
 \label{eq:positive}
\end{equation}
For a positive-probability silence branch, it is
\begin{equation}
 b_i^-=\frac{b_i(1-qh_i)}{1-qm}.
 \label{eq:silence}
\end{equation}
Zero-probability branches impose no condition. For example, when $q=0$, silence leaves the predicted belief unchanged. With $\alpha_t<1$, silence generally preserves positive probability inside $S_t$. Between slots, a public transition matrix predicts the next location belief.

We evaluate posterior concentration through $\|b\|_\infty$, as well as attack hit rate, error, log loss, and calibration. A count of plausible cells alone is not a probability bound. A posterior with many nonzero entries can still place nearly all probability on one cell.

\subsection{Branch-Safe Participation}
The two posterior branches above turn participation into a constrained scalar optimization. Fix a desired posterior threshold $\rho$ and set $c=\max\{\rho,\|b\|_\infty\}$. The local objective is to maximize $q\in[0,1]$ subject to $\|b^y\|_\infty\le c$ for each positive-probability output $y$. Using $c$ prevents a request from being infeasible solely because the predicted belief already exceeds $\rho$. In that situation the rule prevents additional peak concentration relative to the current belief; it cannot restore a previously violated absolute threshold.

If the report posterior in Eq.~\eqref{eq:positive} already exceeds $c$, no positive scalar gate can repair that branch and BSP sets $q=0$. Otherwise, writing $d_i=cm-b_i h_i$, the gate is
\begin{equation}
 q^*=\min\left\{1,\min_{i:d_i>0}\frac{c-b_i}{d_i}\right\},
 \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where an empty inner minimum is $+\infty$; when $m=0$, the output is uninformative and $q^*=1$. Section~\ref{sec:analysis} proves that this is the maximal feasible scalar gate.

The calculation takes $O(N)$ time for one task and stores an $N$-cell belief. Maximizing $q$ maximizes the current expected completion probability $qm$ within this scalar family. It does not optimize the utility of future tasks. In particular, consuming available posterior concentration now can restrict later participation. A nonmyopic scheduler or a mechanism whose gate depends on private location would be a different optimization problem.

\subsection{Robust BSP under Model Uncertainty}
\label{sec:robust}
The closed-form gate above is exact for its declared model, but practical inference may be misspecified. To make that uncertainty explicit, we use a finite public ambiguity set; no distribution-free guarantee is claimed. Let $\mathcal M=\{M_1,\ldots,M_K\}$ be fixed independently of the current private state. Model $M_k$ maintains its own public-history belief $b_t^{(k)}$, transition rule, and availability probability $\alpha_t^{(k)}$. For the current task, let $q_k^*$ be the BSP gate from Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} under that model and let $c_k=\max\{\rho,\|b_t^{(k)}\|_\infty\}$. Robust BSP (R-BSP) uses
\begin{equation}
 q_R^*=\min_{1\le k\le K} q_k^*.
 \label{eq:robust-gate}
\end{equation}
All member beliefs are updated from the same public task and report/silence history; none receives the true location or the gate coin.

R-BSP costs $O(KN)$ per task when each member uses the $N$-cell implementation above. Enlarging $\mathcal M$ can only weakly decrease the feasible gate, so model-set construction is itself a privacy--utility design choice. The formal finite-model guarantee and its out-of-set limitation are analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.

\subsection{Disclosure-Floor-Aware BSP}
\label{sec:dfbsp}
Finite-model robustness addresses model uncertainty, but it does not remove the structural leakage of a truthful positive report. As a result, strict BSP can make fine-region service impossible for a reason that scalar thinning cannot repair. For predicted belief $b$ and region $S$, define the truthful positive-report disclosure floor
\begin{equation}
 \phi(b,S)=\frac{\max_{i\in S}b_i}{\sum_{j\in S}b_j},
 \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
when the denominator is positive. The common availability factor $\alpha$ and any positive scalar gate cancel from this conditional posterior. Hence every truthful successful report from $S$ has posterior peak $\phi(b,S)$, regardless of how small a nonzero $q$ is. Under a uniform prior and an $m$-cell region, $\phi=1/m$; a strict $\rho=0.1$ rule therefore cannot release a truthful success for fewer than ten cells.

To express rather than conceal this service limitation, let $\tau\in[\rho,1]$ be a declared positive-report threshold. DF-BSP uses asymmetric branch caps
\begin{equation}
 c^+_{\mathrm{DF}}=\max\{\tau,\|b\|_\infty\},
 \qquad
 c^-_{\mathrm{DF}}=\max\{\rho,\|b\|_\infty\}.
 \label{eq:dfcap}
\end{equation}
It rejects the task when $\phi(b,S)>c^+_{\mathrm{DF}}$. Otherwise, the positive branch is admissible by construction and the gate is maximized subject to the original BSP silence constraint $\|b^-\|_\infty\le c^-_{\mathrm{DF}}$. Thus the structural relaxation applies only to truthful reports; silence is not allowed to inherit the relaxed report-side threshold.

Robust DF-BSP (RDF-BSP) applies this asymmetric construction independently to every $M_k\in\mathcal M$ and takes the minimum member-wise gate. It is therefore the maximal common scalar gate satisfying all member-specific report ceilings and strict silence caps. Importantly, $\tau>\rho$ relaxes only the truthful-report branch; it is not evidence that the original $\rho$ cap is preserved after reports. We consequently report both violation of the original BSP local cap $\max\{\rho,\|b\|_\infty\}$ and violation of the declared DF branch-specific caps.

Operationally, the client predicts its public-history belief, computes the applicable BSP/R-BSP/DF/RDF gate from public task metadata, samples the private gate coin locally, and releases only a valid report at the common deadline. The observer updates from the same report or silence. The next section establishes the formal guarantees and their scope.

\section{Robustness and Disclosure-Floor Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
The preceding section defines the mechanisms; this section separates what they guarantee from what remains outside the model. Because BSP and its variants are Bayesian output-channel controls rather than cryptographic protocols, their security argument is stated in terms of posterior branches, sequential model assumptions, model mismatch, and the unavoidable information carried by a truthful positive report.

\subsection{Threat Model and Attack Surface}
The platform links outcomes to an account, knows the task regions, deadlines, filtering algorithm, and its parameters, and retains the account's public history. At designated probe slots, it may choose a task region from a finite public library using that history. It does not observe the simulator's actual location, future trajectory, delivery draws, willingness, or deadline-success draws. A prior-only estimator receives no outcomes. A fixed attack uses independently selected regions. An adaptive attack selects the next probe to maximize one-step mutual information under its current belief and the known gate. This greedy attack is a reproducible adversary, not a proof of optimal sequential inference or task selection.

The baseline analyses assume matched client and attacker mobility and observation models. Separate mismatch experiments give the attacker more accurate generating probabilities, while the exploratory GeoLife replay uses a stronger development-derived population prior and a validation-derived mobility estimate. Auxiliary information about an individual's home, routines, network attachment, or other accounts is outside the main model.

\subsection{Branch-Wise Privacy and Maximality}
\begin{proposition}[Maximal scalar gate]
If $m>0$ and $\max_i b_i h_i/m>c$, the only feasible gate is $q^*=0$. Otherwise, Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} is the largest scalar $q\in[0,1]$ for which every positive-probability report and silence posterior has peak at most $c$. When $m=0$, the output is uninformative and $q^*=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every $q>0$ with $m>0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:positive} is independent of $q$. If its peak exceeds $c$, no positive gate can satisfy the report constraint. Otherwise only the silence constraints remain. For a nonzero silence probability, Eq.~\eqref{eq:silence} is at most $c$ exactly when
\begin{equation}
 q(cm-b_i h_i)\le c-b_i\quad\text{for all }i.
\end{equation}
The right side is nonnegative. A nonpositive coefficient on the left imposes no upper bound on $q$; a positive coefficient gives the bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate}. Their intersection with $[0,1]$ yields the stated maximum. If silence has zero probability, the corresponding constraint is vacuous and the same expression admits $q=1$. Finally, $q=0$ is always feasible because the resulting posterior equals $b$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sequential Privacy Guarantee}
\begin{corollary}[Conditional sequential cap]
Suppose the initial belief has peak at most $\rho$, the declared mobility and observation models are correct, all public signals are represented by Eq.~\eqref{eq:output}, and every location transition is doubly stochastic. Then BSP maintains posterior peak at most $\rho$ after every output, including under adaptive task selection measurable from public history.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A doubly stochastic transition makes each predicted probability a convex combination of previous probabilities, so prediction cannot increase the maximum. Proposition~1 bounds every positive-probability observation branch by $\rho$. Induction over slots proves the result. Given the public history, the attacker's adaptive task choice supplies no additional private-state information under the stated measurability assumption.
\end{proof}

A reflecting symmetric grid walk satisfies the transition condition. A general learned human-mobility transition need not. The corollary applies to the attacker's posterior only when its knowledge and model coincide with the declared model.

\subsection{Model-Mismatch Attack and Finite-Model Robustness}
Let $M_C$ denote the client's declared inference model and $M_A$ the attacker's model. When $M_A\neq M_C$, a gate that is feasible for the client posterior need not satisfy the attacker's cap. Incorrect availability, mobility, or prior assumptions can therefore produce an apparently compliant client trace while the attacker's posterior becomes more concentrated. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} evaluates this failure with an informed attacker.

\begin{proposition}[Maximal common finite-model gate]
Within the class of one scalar gate shared by all models, Eq.~\eqref{eq:robust-gate} is the largest $q\in[0,1]$ for which both positive-probability output branches satisfy $\|b_{t,k}^{y}(q)\|_\infty\le c_k$ for every $M_k\in\mathcal M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed model, Proposition~1 gives the complete feasible scalar set as the interval $[0,q_k^*]$: if the positive branch is infeasible then $q_k^*=0$; otherwise the silence inequalities supply only upper bounds on $q$. Requiring all models simultaneously intersects these intervals, giving $[0,\min_k q_k^*]$. Its right endpoint is therefore the maximal common gate.
\end{proof}

Consequently, if the attacker's exact Bayesian model is in $\mathcal M$, its branch constraint is enforced as one member of the intersection; no such conclusion follows for an attacker outside the set.

\subsection{Disclosure-Floor Guarantee}
\begin{proposition}[Maximal asymmetric disclosure-floor-aware scalar gate]
For fixed $(b,S,\alpha,\rho,\tau)$ with $\tau\ge\rho$, DF-BSP returns the largest scalar $q\in[0,1]$ satisfying $\|b^+\|_\infty\le c^+_{\mathrm{DF}}$ for every positive-probability report branch and $\|b^-\|_\infty\le c^-_{\mathrm{DF}}$ for every positive-probability silence branch. When $\tau=\rho$, DF-BSP reduces exactly to BSP.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every $q>0$, the report posterior is independent of $q$ and has peak $\phi(b,S)$. If this exceeds $c^+_{\mathrm{DF}}$, no positive gate is feasible. Otherwise the positive constraint is satisfied for every $q>0$. The remaining silence inequalities are exactly those of Proposition~1 with $c=c^-_{\mathrm{DF}}$, so their intersection with $[0,1]$ is $[0,q^*]$ and the closed-form BSP bound remains maximal. Setting $\tau=\rho$ makes $c^+_{\mathrm{DF}}=c^-_{\mathrm{DF}}=\max\{\rho,\|b\|_\infty\}$ and recovers BSP.
\end{proof}

The result also yields an impossibility within the truthful scalar-gate family: reducing a positive participation probability cannot lower the conditional posterior peak once a successful region report is observed. Fine-region service therefore requires an explicit report-side relaxation such as $\tau$ or a different output semantics.

\subsection{Scope of the Guarantees}
Equation~\eqref{eq:positive} explains why both report and silence must be checked. Scalar thinning changes the probability that a successful report occurs but not its conditional posterior. A single-cell truthful report therefore identifies that cell whenever it is released. Conversely, a report-safe task can still make silence informative because the absence of a report reweights inside and outside states differently. BSP thins only through the public-history scalar gate and sends no separate refusal message.

The same support property rules out interpreting BSP as pure location differential privacy: for a proper region and any nonzero truthful reporting probability, the report event has positive probability inside the region and zero probability outside. Other privacy definitions or mechanisms with altered report semantics remain possible, but they are outside the present guarantee.

These observations define the scope of the formal claims. The robust guarantee is finite-model, and every guarantee is specific to the declared observation channel. If the attacker's exact model belongs to $\mathcal M$, R-BSP enforces its branch constraint; an attacker outside $\mathcal M$ is not covered. Likewise, $\tau>\rho$ explicitly relaxes only the truthful-report branch and therefore cannot be read as preserving the original $\rho$ cap after a successful report. The evaluation below is organized to test both the in-model guarantees and the ways in which these boundaries become visible under mismatch.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}\label{sec:results}
The evaluation proceeds in three layers. We first examine matched-model behavior and recent channel comparators, then introduce model mismatch and finite-set robustness, and finally stress the assumptions that remain outside the formal guarantee, including spatial availability, nonstationarity, deeper probing, and the truthful-report disclosure floor. This progression is intended to separate what the theory guarantees from what the simulations only suggest.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We use three synthetic mobility settings (static, reflecting random walk, and periodic commute) and Microsoft GeoLife trajectories~\cite{geolife2008,geolife2009,geolife2010}. The GeoLife component is a secondary analysis of a publicly released historical dataset; this study performs no new participant recruitment, intervention, or data collection. GeoLife preprocessing scans files in deterministic order and selects the first 48 consecutive in-bounds one-minute bins for each eligible user, without interpolating missing minutes. The rule yields 107 eligible user windows before holdout isolation, split deterministically into 23 development, 17 validation, and 67 test candidates. Because the fitted and replay models consume only the complete 48-slot $8\times8$ state sequence, effective splits are formed by grouping identical model-input paths and assigning each group to the earliest split in development$\rightarrow$validation$\rightarrow$test order. This leaves \GeoDevN{} development, \GeoValN{} validation, and \GeoTestN{} test windows; no complete model-input path crosses the effective splits. Exact GPS-coordinate hashes are retained as a separate provenance audit. Test windows are not used to construct the data-driven ambiguity set. The \(8\times8\) Beijing grid is intentionally coarse; the study evaluates cell inference, not address recovery.

The public task library contains 152 rectangular regions. Normal tasks are exogenous; on the main probe slots, the attacker greedily chooses the region with maximal one-step mutual information from the same public library. This is a reproducible adaptive heuristic, not an optimal long-horizon task policy. The baseline simulator draws delivery, willingness, and timely completion independently with probabilities \(0.9,0.8,0.9\), giving the externally specified scalar availability \(\alpha=0.648\). GeoLife does not contain these task-process variables. Normal tasks and exogenous random draws are shared across policies, while adaptive probes are recomputed because the public history changes. Separate post-review stresses violate the scalar/stationary assumptions through spatially varying availability and nonstationary mobility and also test a two-step planning heuristic.

The main study includes no filtering, random and periodic participation, coarse-region controls, BSP, a success-only ablation, and a per-output KL adaptation of Bayesian privacy~\cite{bayesian}. We also implement task-channel specializations of PML~\cite{pml2024} and PRIVIC~\cite{privic2024}. PML-T optimizes the present truthful binary channel under the published pointwise leakage constraints. PRIVIC-T fits its noisy-location channel with the published BA/IBU/GIBU updates and then maps that release to truthful task completion. These adapters are exploratory channel diagnostics: they change the original application/output semantics and do not inherit the full guarantees of the source methods. We therefore do not use them to claim general superiority over PML or PRIVIC. Comparator equations, author-code checks, and optimization validation are in the Supplementary Material.

\subsection{Data-Driven Ambiguity Set}
A separate synthetic hand-grid R-BSP stress spans broad \((v,\alpha)\) misspecification and is used only to expose finite-model behavior. The GeoLife data-driven extension is distinct: because the replay test users had already been inspected in earlier analyses, it is explicitly post-hoc, while its ambiguity set itself is constructed using only development and validation windows. A coarsened reflecting-walk likelihood uses
\[
 \Pr(\text{cell change}\mid i)=v\,d_i/4,
\]
where \(d_i\) is the number of in-grid neighbors. The likelihood uses only change versus no-change events; destination direction and jump distance are not fitted. Two thousand cluster-bootstrap resamples of development state-path groups generate candidate \(v\) values at the 2.5, 25, 50, 75, and 97.5 percentiles plus the development MLE. Each candidate is paired with a uniform or development-only smoothed population prior. On 2,000 cluster-bootstrap resamples of validation state-path groups, we retain the smallest set covering at least 95\% of predictive-likelihood winners. This selects two population-prior models whose cumulative validation selection mass is 96.95\%, exceeding the 95\% target. The development MLE is \GeoDevMLE{} (95\% bootstrap interval \GeoDevCILow{}--\GeoDevCIHigh{}); the validation point estimate is \GeoValMLE{}. GeoLife has no real task-delivery, willingness, or deadline logs, so \(\alpha=0.648\) remains an explicit protocol parameter rather than a learned quantity.

\subsection{Baselines, Metrics, and Inference Units}
Privacy metrics are MAP cell hit rate, posterior peak, log loss, calibration, and cap-violation rates. The primary nonprivacy metric is \emph{simulated opportunity retention}: completed legitimate tasks divided by legitimate opportunities for which the simulated user is both eligible and available under the stated Bernoulli protocol. It is not a measured user-service rate or production completion probability. To expose fine-region behavior, we additionally report retention for 1--8, 9--31, and 32--64 cell regions and inverse-area-weighted retention
\begin{equation}
 U_{\mathrm{area}}=
 \frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal L}Y_t/|S_t|}
 {\sum_{t\in\mathcal L}\ind\{X_t\in S_t\}D_tW_tL_t/|S_t|}.
 \label{eq:weighted-utility}
\end{equation}
Synthetic point estimates average trajectory rows and their intervals resample seed clusters. GeoLife point estimates average the effective user windows (and their constituent positions), while uncertainty intervals resample complete state-path groups; slots and duplicate model-input paths within a group are therefore not treated as independent clusters. Unless stated otherwise, percentile bootstrap intervals use 1,000 resamples. The data-driven and disclosure-floor experiments are post-hoc extensions because the replay test users had been inspected in earlier analyses; they are not presented as independent confirmatory validation.

\subsection{Effectiveness of BSP and Recent Comparators}
We begin with the matched-model setting, where the mechanism is evaluated under the same observation model used to compute its gate. Under the original uniform-prior attacker, BSP reduces declared-model posterior concentration while retaining nonzero service, although completed tasks are dominated by large regions. A development-only no-output modal-cell predictor already attains \PopulationPriorHit\% hit rate on isolated test positions, showing that a uniform-prior attack is not a deployment upper bound. This gap motivates the stronger population-prior replay attacker used later.

At opportunity retention 0.5 in the static scenario, exploratory envelope interpolation gives BSP MAP hit rate 7.96\%, versus 16.67\% for PML-T and 18.58\% for PRIVIC-T. The paired differences (BSP minus comparator) are \(-8.70\) percentage points (95\% CI: \(-11.33,-5.99\)) and \(-10.62\) percentage points (95\% CI: \(-14.76,-4.70\)), respectively. Fig.~\ref{fig:recent-comparators} visualizes this matched-retention static comparison. Walk, commute, and replay comparisons do not show a consistent advantage over both recent comparators, and some matched-retention intervals cross zero. Because the policy envelopes are selected from evaluation results, these are exploratory comparisons rather than independently tuned superiority claims.

\subsection{Model-Mismatch Attack}
Matched-model performance does not answer the more difficult question of what happens when the observer knows a better model than the client. In the informed synthetic stress at \(\rho=0.1\), nominal BSP is fragile: local-cap violations reach 12.6\% when the client underestimates availability, 2.6\% when it overestimates availability, 2.8\% when it underestimates movement, and 14.6\% when it overestimates movement. These failures expose the model dependence of nominal BSP and motivate the robust extension; they do not contradict the model-conditional theorem.

\subsection{Finite-Model Robustness}
R-BSP is designed precisely for this type of declared model uncertainty. A nine-model hand-grid stress contains the informed attacker's exact \((v,\alpha)=(0.3,0.648)\) model. At \(\rho=0.1\), it records zero local-cap violations in the four misspecified conditions because the informed attacker's exact generating model is included in the set, but it retains only about 39.7\% of externally completable normal-task opportunities. Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness-plot} visualizes this robustness--utility trade-off. The zero-violation result is consistent with the finite-set construction, while the 39.7\% retention quantifies its utility cost; neither result implies robustness beyond the specified set.

A separate boundary run fixes the same model set and changes the correct attacker model. An outside-set availability value \(\alpha=0.98\) restores a 6.93\% local-cap violation rate (95\% cluster-bootstrap interval 5.70--8.31\%). Interior unlisted values produce zero or rare violations in this finite sample. The guarantee is therefore explicitly finite-model. Full stress tables and boundary conditions are in the Supplementary Material.

\subsection{Sensitivity Beyond the Baseline Model}
Finite-set robustness still leaves a second question: how sensitive is the mechanism to assumptions that are not represented in the ambiguity set? We first examine discretization. The original sensitivity study varies the grid resolution in the synthetic walk setting. With BSP at $\rho=0.1$, the $4\times4$ grid gives a 7.54\% MAP-cell hit rate and 10.85\% simulated opportunity retention, whereas the $16\times16$ grid gives 2.57\% hit and 81.03\% retention. These numbers are not directly comparable as physical localization errors because changing the grid changes the cell size, state-space prior, and task library. They instead show that the privacy--retention frontier is resolution dependent.

Additional targeted post-review stresses are summarized in full in the Supplementary Material. Under a synthetic spatial availability field ($0.35$ on one half of the grid and $0.85$ on the other), the client still uses scalar $\alpha=0.648$ while the attacker knows the spatial field. BSP then violates the attacker's local cap on 6.56\% of slots; the nine-model R-BSP reduces, but does not eliminate, the rate to 0.47\%. When the true/attacker movement probability alternates between $0.05$ and $0.8$ every eight slots while the client remains stationary at $0.3$, BSP records 2.34\% local-cap violations and R-BSP records none in this finite sample. These results are deliberate model-outside-set stresses and do not extend the theorem.

We also replace every normal task by a probe and compare the main one-step mutual-information greedy rule with a two-step beam look-ahead that scores current information gain plus the branch-probability-weighted best next-slot gain. In this tested mismatch, the deeper heuristic does \emph{not} dominate the one-step rule: BSP local-cap violations are 25.47\% for one-step greedy and 14.22\% for the two-step heuristic, with similar MAP hit rates (9.04\% and 9.23\%). R-BSP records no local-cap violation for either heuristic. Thus the additional test broadens task-planning sensitivity but still does not establish protection against an optimal long-horizon attacker.

\subsection{Fine-Grained Service under Disclosure Floors}
Robustness addresses model uncertainty, but it does not solve the separate service loss caused by the truthful-report disclosure floor. Table~\ref{tab:dfbsp} therefore quantifies the explicit alternative: relax the report-side ceiling while keeping the silence branch at the original BSP cap. \subsection{Computation and Deployment Cost}
For an \(n\)-cell state space, BSP evaluates a constant number of dot products and componentwise maxima and is therefore \(O(n)\) per candidate task. R-BSP repeats the same feasibility calculation across a finite ambiguity set of size \(M\), giving \(O(Mn)\) work before taking the common gate. The KL comparator additionally performs an iterative one-dimensional feasibility search, whereas PML-T evaluates the specialized task-channel constraints and PRIVIC-T requires separate offline channel fitting. The repository records Python wall-clock timings for implementation profiling, but absolute values vary with hardware, runtime, and batch context and are therefore not treated as portable performance claims. Probe search, posterior diagnostics, loading, communication, smartphone latency, energy consumption, and cryptographic execution time are outside the reported online gate computation and were not measured as deployment benchmarks.

\subsection{Mobile-System Implications and Limitations}
For a mobile sensing client, BSP can be inserted between local task eligibility and report upload without changing the platform's task-assignment protocol or emitting false reports. The client must maintain its declared location belief, receive the public task region, sampling slot, and deadline, and update the belief from the same public outcome history available to the platform. At the protocol-design level this requires no false reports and no change to task assignment, but compatibility with a production platform has not been demonstrated. The modeling boundary is therefore operational: richer acknowledgments, network-side metadata, personalized side information, or platform-specific availability signals must be represented in the observation model before the stated guarantee applies. A finer state grid increases both belief-state memory and the $O(n)$ gate cost; R-BSP additionally scales linearly with the number of ambiguity models. These are integration costs, not measured smartphone energy or end-to-end latency results.

Taken together, the evaluation supports a deliberately narrow conclusion. A public-history participation rule can reduce inference under its declared model while retaining some large-region task opportunities, and checking both observable branches yields a clear model-conditional bound. The strongest recent-comparator evidence appears in the static setting; walk, commute, and replay results do not show a consistent advantage over both recent comparators. Likewise, coarse replay improvements under a uniform-prior observer should not be interpreted as an upper bound on practical inference accuracy. The remaining limitations explain why these results should be read as a mechanism study rather than a deployment claim.

The first limitation is model dependence. Nominal BSP relies on one declared belief model. R-BSP turns this limitation into an explicit finite ambiguity-set guarantee by intersecting member-wise constraints, and the hand-grid stress test shows the expected removal of local-cap violations when the informed attacker's exact model is included. The development/validation construction replaces the hand-grid choice for replay mobility and prior uncertainty with a reproducible selection rule. The smallest set meeting the 95\% target contains two population-prior mobility candidates and covers 96.95\% of validation-bootstrap winners. Strict data-driven R-BSP then retains only \GeoStrictRet\% of isolated replay opportunities. Thus ``data-driven'' does not mean ``low cost.'' The boundary experiment also shows that an outside-set availability model at $\alpha=0.98$ can restore violations. Moreover, $\alpha$ itself is not learned because the trajectory dataset contains no task-availability observations. A deployment would need task-process data, distribution-shift handling, and prospective evaluation of the set-construction rule.

A second limitation concerns the observation channel itself. The model omits informative measurement contents, acknowledgments, payment records, network observations, and within-slot timing. Common-deadline release is a protocol requirement, not an implemented property of a real platform. General collection windows need a state describing visits or acquisition time rather than only the current sampled cell. The on-time probability sweep does not replace such a model.

A third limitation is empirical coverage. The data are short, selected, historical, and coarse. One 48-minute window per user does not describe a full day; excluding users without consecutive samples can change the mobility mix. \ReplayStaticWindows{} isolated test windows have no observed cell change, and movements within a cell of roughly 4.2 km by 4.3 km at the study latitude are invisible at this resolution. Tasks are simulated on public movement traces. The experiments estimate neither the prevalence of current attacks nor the probability of identifying a home. The development-fitted no-output diagnostic quantifies one weakness of the uniform prior. The exploratory replay reruns adaptive probes with a development-only smoothed population prior and validation-derived mobility estimate. Because the replay test windows had already been inspected in preceding analyses, these results are not treated as independent confirmatory evidence.

A fourth limitation is adversarial scope. The main attack is greedy with a fixed probe budget. The post-review two-step look-ahead broadens this sensitivity check but does not approximate an optimal long-horizon policy and did not dominate one-step greedy in the tested mismatch. The spatial-availability and nonstationary-mobility stresses further show that model structure matters outside the declared set. Coordinated accounts and personalized auxiliary priors remain untested. Strict truthful reporting has a structural granularity cost: scalar thinning cannot reduce the conditional leakage of a success. DF-BSP makes the resulting service relaxation explicit through $\tau$ and improves fine-region retention while keeping the silence branch at the original BSP cap; it changes only the truthful-report privacy contract whenever $\tau>\rho$. The inverse-area utility is deliberately diagnostic rather than an economic welfare model, and the tested $\tau$ values do not identify an optimal operating point. A real deployment would need application-specific task values or measurement reconstruction quality and a policy for choosing $\tau$. Independent account repetitions do not constitute a multi-user allocation or platform-capacity benchmark.

Finally, the comparative evidence is necessarily exploratory. Comparisons depend on finite parameter grids and interpolation over observed operating points. Bootstrap intervals condition on the selected traces, tasks, and clustering, without resolving distribution shift or multiple testing. The KL and recent-comparator analyses are exploratory. Recent policy envelopes are selected on the evaluation set; their bootstrap intervals describe this selection procedure rather than validating an independently tuned policy. PRIVIC uses one noisy training seed and some IBU/GIBU loops reach their iteration limits; small likelihood gaps do not establish distribution recovery. Its true-eligibility task adapter changes the published output semantics. HECTA differs in observation semantics, trust assumptions, and privacy objectives; the present study does not provide a head-to-head system evaluation or claim an exhaustive novelty assessment.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
The experimental code, configurations, seeds, comparator-validation fixtures, tests, generated numerical tables, and frozen aggregate/per-run evidence are published in the version-pinned \href{https://github.com/js110/Branch-Safe/tree/2dfaeeced5bb162203fa689b482d229371a17671}{Branch-Safe artifact repository}~\cite{bspartifact2026}. Its \texttt{docs/evidence\_map.md} and \texttt{docs/source\_manifest.json} map the reported results to their inputs and verify byte-identical exports from the frozen source. Complete historical event-level logs and the manuscript sources remain \href{https://github.com/js110/Bpaper/tree/2cc94ff6e7c4696dc364392dc19c8137d0cb7e65}{archived with the original study}; they are not silently claimed to be duplicated in the new code repository. Raw Microsoft GeoLife data must be obtained separately and are not redistributed. Ground-truth simulator states are used for evaluation but are unavailable to the modeled observer, and the effective GeoLife splits can be reconstructed from the recorded preprocessing and model-input isolation rules.

The automated suite contains 35 tests. Core tests cover posterior branches, nominal/robust/disclosure-floor gate bounds and reductions, an informed-attacker R-BSP integration case, the coarsened mobility estimator, KL feasibility, transition conservation, clustered-estimator weighting, and boundary cases. Pipeline and comparator tests cover duplicate or expired tasks, missing minutes, split separation, paired normal tasks, numerical replay, BA/IBU conformance to the PRIVIC author code, PML optimality against a general linear program, vector-valued observation channels, and policy-envelope agreement with a mixture linear program. Superseded exploratory or interrupted outputs are excluded from the current evidence set and remain recoverable through version history; completed evidence runs used by the manuscript contain no recorded simulation failures.

\section{Conclusion}
Sequential regional task outcomes can reveal location even when precise coordinates never leave the device, because both a truthful report and its absence can become evidence over time. BSP addresses this channel directly by providing a maximal scalar gate for both posterior branches under explicit model assumptions, and R-BSP extends that construction to a declared finite model set. The experiments show both sides of this design choice. Development/validation-based model selection makes the ambiguity set more reproducible, yet it does not make the guarantee distribution-free: spatial-availability and nonstationary stresses expose residual or setting-dependent violations outside the modeled assumptions, and the outside-set boundary experiment restores violations under a more extreme availability value. At the same time, strict robust caps can be conservative, with the isolated GeoLife replay retaining only \GeoStrictRet\% of simulated eligible-and-available opportunities. DF-BSP and RDF-BSP make the resulting structural trade-off explicit by introducing a separate report-side threshold $\tau$, thereby recovering fine-region opportunity retention only when the report branch is deliberately relaxed. The comparator and GeoLife analyses remain exploratory rather than confirmatory superiority tests. The resulting contribution is therefore a mechanism-and-analysis framework for a specified binary observation channel under finite model uncertainty---not a deployed mobile system, a measured real-user service improvement, or a guarantee against arbitrary auxiliary knowledge or optimal long-horizon task selection.
